Why We Avoid Them

Awareness is the first step to change.

We all avoid conversations sometimes.
It doesn't mean we're weak or flawed. It means we're human.

This week is about noticing what gets in the way for you — with curiosity and self-compassion. The more we understand ourselves, the more choice we have.

There's no pressure to fix anything right now.Just notice. That's enough.

Self-Compassion First

Avoiding a conversation is not a character flaw. It's usually you trying to protect something important.

Today, the goal isn't to change.
It's to understand.
Understanding leads to better choices.
1
What conversation have you been avoiding (or have avoided)?
Think of a conversation that has been on your mind, or that you've put off.
2
What stops you from going towards it?
Look at the list below. Which of these feel most familiar for you? You can choose more than one.
3
When has this shown up before?
Think of a time (not necessarily this exact conversation) when you avoided speaking up or having a difficult conversation.
What happened?
What was going through your mind?
What were you hoping would happen?
How did it work out?
Patterns repeat so we notice. Not so we judge. So we understand.
4
What are you protecting?
When we avoid a conversation, it's usually because we're trying to protect something that matters to us.

On the Other Side

Better conversations lead to:

  • More honesty and trust
  • Less stress and mental load
  • Clearer decisions and boundaries
  • Stronger relationships
  • A life that feels more aligned with who you are
Remember
Avoidance makes sense.
Awareness creates choice.
Choice creates change.
This Month
We'll explore what gets in the way, get clearer on what matters, and build the skills and confidence to have difficult conversations in a way that fits you.
This is the start of something powerful.
You don't have to have it all figured out. You just have to begin.
