Identity Is Not Fixed · Week 3

Who Am I Now?

What matters to me — not just what I've absorbed.

We've explored the identity I've learned (Week 1) and the stories I inherited (Week 2). This week is about me — what I actually value, what I'm becoming, and what matters most right now. Not who I was told to be. Not who I had to be. Who I choose to be.

✏️ This tool works in phases. Each phase builds on the last — the questions in Phase 1 contain the answer to Phase 3. Work through them in order. Your answers auto-save as you type.

1
Phase 1 — What the evidence says
Before we name any values, let's look at the evidence. Your choices, sacrifices, and reactions already reveal what matters most to you — you just haven't named it yet. Answer these four questions honestly.
⚖️
The decision test
Describe a decision you made in the last three months that cost you something — time, money, comfort, or a relationship. What did you choose, and why?
🎁
The sacrifice test
What have you willingly given up in the last year? What does that tell you about what matters more to you than the thing you gave up?
🔥
The anger / frustration test
What's made you genuinely angry or frustrated recently — not surface irritation, but something that felt like a real violation? What does that reaction reveal about what you hold dear?
⭐
The pride test
When have you felt most like yourself recently — not most successful, not most approved of, but most you? What was happening, and what does that moment tell you?
2
Phase 2 — What patterns appear
Read back what you just wrote. Don't analyse it — just read it as if someone else wrote it. What themes keep appearing?

Reading the evidence…

What words, themes, or qualities keep showing up across your four answers? What seems to be driving your choices, even when it's inconvenient or costly?

Now look at the value families below. Only select a family if you can point to specific evidence in what you wrote above. This is not about what sounds good — it's about what's actually there.
3
Phase 3 — Name it in your own words
For each value you identify, name it the way you would say it — not a dictionary word, but the lived version. Then write your personal definition: what does this actually look like in your choices?
4
Phase 4 — The reality check
For each value, rate honestly: in the last 30 days, how consistently has this actually driven your choices? Not how much you care about it — how often has it shown up in your behaviour?
A score of 7+ means this is a lived value — it's actually operating in your life. Below 7 means it may be aspirational — you care about it, but something else is taking priority. Both are useful to know.

Complete Phase 3 first — your values will appear here.
