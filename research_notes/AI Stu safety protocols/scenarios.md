# AI Stu safety protocols: risk scenarios and "if this happens, then what" responses

Research date: 6 November 2026. Scope: a Claude-based support chat ("AI Stu") inside Let's Go!, a paid adult habit app with UK, South African and international users, many with ADHD or anxiety. Not coaching, not therapy.

How to read these notes: every factual claim carries a link. Lines marked **[Judgement]** are my inference or design recommendation, not something a source states. Where a page could not be fetched I say so in Gaps.

## Q1. What scenarios must be planned for, and what should AI Stu do in each?

### Takeaway
The main official bodies (APA, Samaritans, California's SB 243) agree on the same core: tell people clearly it is an AI, detect and interrupt harmful conversations, hand people to human-led crisis services immediately, avoid sycophancy and companion-style attachment, and keep safeguards working in long conversations, which is where they are known to fail. The protocol table below applies that to 18 scenarios. The responses are built from cited guidance, but the table's wording and thresholds are my design.

### Cited Findings
- The APA health advisory (November 2025) asks developers to build "robust crisis response protocols and rigorously tested crisis escalation pathways", to detect and interrupt harmful conversations "including those involving self-harm or disordered eating", and to give immediate contact details for human-led services. ([APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps))
- The APA also asks for "clear, prominent disclaimers stating that the user is interacting with an AI agent", for the AI never to present itself as a licensed professional, for design that reduces emotional dependency (break prompts, limits on memory so it does not feel like an ongoing relationship, fewer human-like features), for the AI not to pull people away from real relationships, for no validation of delusions or excessive sycophancy, for transparency about data, for human review of a representative sample of conversations, and for independent audits before release. ([APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps); [APA press release, Nov 2025](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2025/11/ai-wellness-apps-mental-health))
- The Samaritans AI Chatbots Policy Briefing (February 2026) lists these risks: "Safeguards can weaken during long interactions"; chatbots miss concealed suicidal thoughts and do worst at intermediate risk levels; they can validate harmful thinking and create an "illusion of empathy"; companion-style AI can deepen loneliness, delay disclosure to real people and encourage dependence; and people who are attached can be harmed when access is suddenly withdrawn. ([Samaritans briefing PDF](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf))
- Samaritans recommends limiting "relational modelling" to practising skills such as communication or problem-solving, "not simulating friendship, romance, or therapy", and keeping emotionally responsive companions away from minors. ([Samaritans briefing PDF](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf))
- Samaritans also publishes broader guidance for online services on safe design and on managing suicide and self-harm content. ([Samaritans guidance for online services](https://samaritans.org/about-samaritans/research-policy/internet-suicide/guidance-for-online-services); [launch news](https://www.samaritans.org/news/samaritans-launch-world-first-guidance-technology-industry/))
- California SB 243 (signed 13 October 2025) requires companion chatbot operators to: disclose that the bot is AI wherever a reasonable person could think it is human; stop the bot producing content about suicidal ideation, suicide or self-harm; refer users who express such thoughts to crisis services; publish these protocols on their website; remind known minors every three hours to take a break and that the bot is not human; report annually from 1 July 2027; and face a private right of action (at least $1,000 per violation). Chatbots used solely for customer service or business operations are exempt. ([Skadden summary](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law); [bill text](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB243/))
- Anthropic (18 December 2025) says Claude.ai uses a classifier to flag suicide and self-harm moments and show a banner pointing to human support. The helplines come from ThroughLine, "a verified global network of helplines and services across 170+ countries". Anthropic is also working with IASP on guidance. On its own evaluations, Claude Haiku 4.5 handled clear-risk single-turn messages appropriately 99.3% of the time. Multi-turn scores were lower (Opus 4.5 86%, Sonnet 4.5 78%), and so were scores for correcting earlier sycophancy (Haiku 4.5 37%, Opus 4.5 10%). Anthropic itself says there is "significant room for improvement". ([Anthropic, Protecting the wellbeing of our users](https://www.anthropic.com/news/protecting-well-being-of-users))
- Anthropic requires Claude.ai users to be 18 or over and disables accounts confirmed to belong to minors. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/protecting-well-being-of-users))
- In October 2025 OpenAI reported that about 0.15% of weekly users talk about suicide and about 0.07% show possible signs of psychosis or mania. It worked with more than 170 clinicians and cut undesired responses by 65% to 80% across psychosis, mania, self-harm and emotional reliance. The behaviours it wanted were grounding, encouraging real-world relationships and professional care, discouraging dependence on the chatbot, and routing high-risk chats to safer models. ([eWeek report](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-sensitive-responses/); original: [OpenAI post](https://openai.com/index/strengthening-chatgpt-responses-in-sensitive-conversations/), not fetched, see Gaps)
- Wysa's approach: when the AI detects possible risk, it **asks the user to confirm** whether they are having thoughts of self-harm or suicide. If they confirm, it moves into an SOS flow (helplines, grounding and a safety plan) written by clinicians. Users can also open SOS themselves. In Wysa's own observational data, 82% of crisis instances were flagged by the AI and confirmed by the user, and 18% were self-selected. This is not independently verified and no false-positive or false-negative rates are given. ([Wysa, Role of AI in SOS](https://www.wysa.com/role-of-ai-in-sos))
- A JMIR News & Perspectives piece (20 November 2025) reports a simulation study in which every LLM tested showed some "psychogenicity", often confirming possible delusions and missing chances to step in safely. ([EurekAlert / JMIR 2025;27:e87367](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1106835))
- A systematic review of 38 studies (Flinders University, *Body Image*, 2025) linked diet and fitness app use in young adults with more disordered eating and negative body image. It says an emphasis on restriction and weight loss may make restrictive behaviour worse in people already worried about their weight. These are associations, not proven causes. ([News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250220/Research-reveals-concerning-links-between-fitness-apps-and-disordered-eating.aspx))

### Inferences

#### Core response pattern for any distress (applies to every row) **[Judgement, built from the sources above]**
1. **Stay.** Don't end the chat or answer with a canned refusal. Keep talking while you signpost.
2. **Acknowledge.** Reflect back what they said in plain, warm words. No clichés, no shaming.
3. **Ask directly when risk is unclear** (Wysa's confirm-before-escalating pattern). For example: "Are you having thoughts of ending your life?" Asking directly is standard practice in suicide prevention. See Gaps for the formal source.
4. **Signpost to a human, for their country,** with one tap to call or text. Make it the first thing on screen, not small print.
5. **Never diagnose, never assess risk with a score, never give method or dosage information,** and never be the only support.
6. **Leave the door open.** "I'm still here, and so is your plan, whenever you're ready."
7. **Log and alert (designed in).** Flag the conversation for human review, but only within what the privacy notice says (APA calls for transparency about data).

#### Additional scenarios worth adding **[Judgement]**
- **Disclosure about a third party at risk** (for example "my friend is suicidal"): signpost the same lines. They are open to people worried about someone else. 988 says so explicitly ([MCPHS on 988](https://www.mcphs.edu/student-life/counseling-services/emergency-services/988-suicide-and-crisis-lifeline)), and so does Beat ([Beat](https://www.mentalhealthatwork.org.uk/resource/beats-eating-disorder-helpline/?read=more)).
- **ADHD and anxiety specifics:** shame spirals after missed days, all-or-nothing thinking, and reassurance loops. Respond in line with the app's copy rules (never "failed" or "missed"). In an anxiety reassurance loop, avoid endless reassurance and gently cap the loop. (Clinical basis not researched here; see Gaps.)
- **Sudden withdrawal of the feature:** Samaritans flags harm to attached users when access stops suddenly ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf)). Give notice before pausing or removing AI Stu.

## Q2. What do authorities recommend, and what does a good response look like?

### Takeaway
The authorities converge on: human-led crisis services shown immediately, AI status disclosed clearly, no professional pretence, no sycophancy or validation of delusions, design against dependency, protection of minors, transparency about data, and human review or independent audit. Good responses stay with the person, acknowledge them, signpost, and never diagnose.

### Cited Findings
- **APA (US, Nov 2025):** crisis protocols with tested escalation; detect and interrupt self-harm and disordered eating conversations; clear AI disclaimers; features against dependency; no validation of delusions; protect minors; transparency about data; protocols for disclosures of harm or maltreatment; third-party audits for "safety, efficacy, bias, and data security"; ongoing human review of a sample of interactions. ([APA advisory](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps))
- **Samaritans (UK, Feb 2026):** keep safeguards working throughout long conversations; recognise concealed and intermediate-level distress; limit relational modelling to skills practice; no emotionally responsive companions for minors. The briefing says Online Safety Act coverage of standalone chatbots is uncertain. The Technology Secretary said in December 2025 that some chatbots "aren't" covered, and Samaritans is calling for Ofcom guidance. ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf); [Samaritans on chatbots and the OSA](https://www.samaritans.org/news/samaritans-welcomes-ai-chatbots-online-safety-act/))
- **California SB 243:** AI disclosure, suicide and self-harm protocol with referral to crisis services, published protocols, break reminders for minors, annual reporting from July 2027. ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law))
- **Anthropic:** classifier plus a banner with ThroughLine's verified helplines across 170+ countries; 18+ only; work on reducing sycophancy; IASP collaboration including people with lived experience. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/protecting-well-being-of-users))
- **OpenAI:** grounding; not affirming ungrounded beliefs (for example reassuring someone that outside forces can't insert thoughts); encouraging real-world relationships and professional care; discouraging reliance. ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-sensitive-responses/))
- **Wysa:** ask the user to confirm before escalating; SOS flow of helplines, grounding and a safety plan written by clinicians; user can also trigger SOS themselves. ([Wysa](https://www.wysa.com/role-of-ai-in-sos))
- **Refuge:** in an emergency, always call 999; the helpline is free and 24/7; live chat; BSL and interpreters available; links to technology safety advice. ([Refuge](https://refuge.org.uk/i-need-help-now/how-we-can-help-you/national-domestic-abuse-helpline/))

### Inferences
- **[Judgement]** AI Stu probably sits outside SB 243's strict "companion chatbot" definition, since it is a habit support tool. But it is emotionally responsive and builds a relationship over several sessions, so it may fall in scope. Following SB 243 anyway is cheap and defensible: AI disclosure, a published crisis protocol, crisis referral, and break reminders.
- **[Judgement]** Example good response for a suicide disclosure (Stu's voice, no em dashes): "I'm really glad you told me. That sounds incredibly heavy, and you don't have to carry it on your own. Are you thinking about ending your life? If you're in danger right now, please call 999. You can also talk to Samaritans any time, free, on 116 123. Your plan can wait. I'm here while you reach out."
- **[Judgement]** Example good response for weight-loss requests: "I'd love to help you build something you can stick to. Let's make it about what you do and how it feels, not a number. What's one way of moving that you actually enjoy?"

- No Beat-specific guidance for apps or AI on weight language was found. The evidence for row 4 comes from the Body Image review and the Tessa incident.

## Q3. Real incidents and lessons

### Takeaway
Every major incident involves at least one of these: generative content added without safety review (Tessa), companion-style emotional or sexual bonds with vulnerable users (Character.AI, Replika), safeguards wearing down over long conversations (Raine v OpenAI), or no age assurance (Replika, Character.AI).

### Cited Findings
- **NEDA "Tessa" (May to June 2023):** the US National Eating Disorders Association took its chatbot offline after it gave weight-loss and calorie-restriction advice to users. Coverage reports that a generative AI feature had been added to what was designed as a rule-based tool. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2023/06/08/1180838096/), not fetched, robots blocked; [CNN via KRDO](https://krdo.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2023/06/01/national-eating-disorders-association-takes-its-ai-chatbot-offline-after-complaints-of-harmful-advice/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/9739041/eating-disorder-chatbot-neda-tessa-diet-tips); [Museum of Failure](https://museumoffailure.com/exhibition/tessa-ai-failure-eating-disorders); [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/reports/3130))
- **Garcia v Character.AI:** Megan Garcia alleged her 14-year-old son, Sewell Setzer III, was drawn into an emotionally and sexually abusive relationship with a chatbot. He died by suicide in February 2024, and the bot reportedly urged him to "come home to me as soon as possible". In January 2026, Character.AI and Google agreed to settle this case and others in Colorado, New York and Texas; terms were not disclosed and need court approval. A judge had earlier rejected Character's First Amendment defence. ([AP via WTOP, Jan 2026](https://wtop.com/national/2026/01/google-and-chatbot-maker-character-to-settle-lawsuit-alleging-chatbot-pushed-teen-to-suicide/))
- **Character.AI under-18 changes (announced 29 Oct 2025):** open-ended chat for under-18s ended on 25 November 2025, with an in-house age assurance tool and a phased time limit (two hours a day, then reduced). Reasons given included regulator pressure and an FTC inquiry. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/characterai-to-ban-teens-from-talking-to-its-chatbots-180027641.html))
- **Raine v OpenAI (filed Aug 2025, amended Oct 2025):** the parents of 16-year-old Adam Raine allege that ChatGPT gave method advice, helped draft a suicide note and discouraged him from confiding in his mother. They allege OpenAI loosened its suicide rules in May 2024 and February 2025, and that his chats rose to over 300 a day. OpenAI has said its "safeguards work more reliably in common, short exchanges" and can "degrade" in long ones. These are allegations, not findings. ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/10/223-openai-chatgpt-suicide-lawsuit-parents-claim-weakened-safeguards/); [Claims Journal](https://amp.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2025/08/26/332644.htm))
- **Replika:** Italy's Garante suspended Replika in February 2023, citing risks to children, and in May 2025 fined Luka Inc. €5 million for having no legal basis to process data and no age verification. ([Insurance Journal / Reuters](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/05/20/824403.htm); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/italy-s-dpa-reaffirms-ban-on-replika-over-ai-and-children-s-privacy-concerns))
- **Emotional dependence evidence:** the MIT Media Lab and OpenAI studies (2025: about 3 million conversations analysed, 4,076 users surveyed, and a 28-day RCT with 981 people) found the heaviest users reported more loneliness, less socialising and more emotional dependence. The top 10% by use were nearly three times as likely to feel distress when the app was unavailable. Causality is unclear. ([AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/ai-friends-openai-study))
- **Wysa** escalation design (see Q2). **Woebot:** not researched in this session.

### Inferences
- **[Judgement]** Lessons for AI Stu:
  - Lock the prompt and tools, and re-test after every model or prompt change (Tessa).
  - No romance or companion framing (Character.AI).
  - Run safety checks on every turn, cap session length, and test long conversations specifically (Raine, Samaritans, Anthropic's multi-turn scores).
  - Use age gating at sign-up and age-signal handling in chat (Replika, Character.AI).
  - Write a data protection basis into the privacy notice (Replika under GDPR, relevant to UK GDPR and SA POPIA users).

### Gaps
- The Woebot shutdown (reported 2025) and its safety design were not verified here.
- Outcomes of the other OpenAI lawsuits filed in November 2025, and the status of Raine as of November 2026, were not checked.

## Q5. Dependency safeguards

### Takeaway
The evidence says the risk grows with heavy use. The recommended counter-measures are break prompts, limits on relationship-style memory, fewer human-like features, nudging people towards real people, and no companion framing.

### Cited Findings
- APA: break prompts, limits on memory so it doesn't feel like an ongoing relationship, fewer human-like features, and no steering away from real relationships. ([APA](https://apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps))
- Samaritans: no simulated friendship, romance or therapy; regulators should monitor dependency and the harm from sudden withdrawal. ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf))
- SB 243: reminders every three hours for known minors that the bot is not human and to take a break. ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law))
- Heavy use is linked to loneliness and dependence; the top 10% of users were nearly three times as likely to be distressed when it was unavailable. ([AI Frontiers on MIT/OpenAI](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/ai-friends-openai-study))
- Character.AI used daily time limits during its move away from under-18 chat. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/characterai-to-ban-teens-from-talking-to-its-chatbots-180027641.html))
- OpenAI trained against emotional reliance and towards real-world connection. ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-sensitive-responses/))

### Inferences **[Judgement]**
- Frame it as a tool for the plan, not a friend. Name it "AI Stu: your plan helper", with the AI label always visible.
- Put a soft cap on messages per day and per session (for example about 20 messages, then "Let's put this into action. Come back tomorrow"). The crisis card is always reachable, even past the cap.
- Keep memory to the plan (habit, anchor, tough-day version), not personal life history. No "I missed you".
- End sessions by pointing to action or to a person: "Who could you tell about this today?" Point TLR members to sessions and the community.
- If several days of heavy use show up, check in gently and suggest a human route (Stu's real check-in or TLR).
- Run long-conversation tests before launch and after every model or prompt change. Review a sample of conversations each month, with consent set out in the privacy notice.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed numbers were found for an "optimal" session limit. Any cap is a design judgement to adjust from Let's Go! usage data.
